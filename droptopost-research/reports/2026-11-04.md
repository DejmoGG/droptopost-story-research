**HE TELLS EVERY CUSTOMER NOT TO BUY HIS SCARVES. SO MANY PEOPLE CAME TO BUY THEM THAT HE HAD TO SHUT THE SHOP.**

Clips of his patter spread in late October and drew 1 million to 5 million views. Queues formed before opening, even on weekdays.
- **The turn:** On 28 Oct he asked people to stop rushing over. It didn't work. On 31 Oct he closed the stall: "I want to make money, but not like this," citing safety and the crowds he couldn't handle alone. Neighbouring traders worried about the crush; one outlet wrote that he's "feeding all of Namdaemun Market".

### 2026-11-04-B-2 · Candidate 7/10 · Older (June 2026) · Evidence High
**A CAR DEALER'S "SALES AGENT" OFFERED A CUSTOMER $27,162.79 FOR HIS BMW. THEN THE DEALER ADMITTED THE AGENT WAS AN AI, AND TRIED TO TAKE IT BACK.**
- **What happened:** Zack Giacomelli wanted to sell his 2021 BMW X3 back to BMW Toronto. In the website chat, an agent named "Quinn" negotiated and offered C$27,162.79, exactly what he still owed. Then a human salesperson called: Quinn wasn't a person, it was an AI that had mixed up his loan payoff with the purchase price. The new offer was about C$20,000.
- **The turn:** CBC News called the dealership. It reversed itself and honoured the original C$27,162.79 "to do right" by the customer.
- **Payoff:** The dealership said buybacks would be handled only by humans from now on. The mistake cost it about C$7,000 (reported).
- **Why anyone would watch:** A robot made you a deal, then its bosses said "it doesn't count, it's a robot."
- **Why a business owner cares:** Whatever your chatbot promises, you own it. One phone call from a journalist decided the price.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 7 · Narrative 7 · Business 9 · Evidence 8 · Freshness 5
- **Result:** **Verified** (CBC via multiple outlets): offer revoked, then reinstated; dealer moved buybacks to humans. Cost ~C$7,000 (reported, Carscoops/Jalopnik framing).
