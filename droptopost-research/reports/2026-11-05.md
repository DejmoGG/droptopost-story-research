- **What happened:** In late 2023 Bryan Mansell consigned his father's Star Wars Lego collection (about $200,000 by his estimate; store records put it near $100,000) at a Bricks & Minifigs franchise store in Oregon. The store changed hands and the collection disappeared. In May 2026 he asked YouTuber Benjamin Schneider ("Reckless Ben") for help; Ben's series, starting 21 May with "I tracked down the thief who stole $200,000 of LEGO", drew millions of views and a mock company called "We Steal From Old People".
- **The turn:** The franchisor, based in Utah, sued Ben and others for over $300,000 (defamation, racketeering, harassment) and got a judge to order the videos down. American Fork police arrested him twice; he was charged with misdemeanour stalking and targeted residential picketing after complaints from one of the franchise owners. The police search found no Lego.
- **Payoff:** On 4 Jun 2026 Bricks & Minifigs closed the Salem store and cut ties with its two franchise owners, citing "gross negligence" in a franchise review and a "devastating social media campaign". On 19 Aug it settled with the Mansell family; Bryan Mansell said he received the Lego sets on 2 Sep. Patreon's CEO publicly refused to take Ben's page down. The case against Ben was still unresolved in the latest reports.
- **Why anyone would watch:** An old man's toy collection, a missing fortune in plastic bricks, and the detective ends up in handcuffs.
- **Why a business owner cares:** A franchise ignored one customer's complaint for two years; one creator's videos closed the store in two weeks, and suing him made it bigger.
