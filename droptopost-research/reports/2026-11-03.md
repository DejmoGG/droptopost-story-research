### 2026-11-03-B-1 · Story 92 · Recent · Evidence Medium
**AN 80-YEAR-OLD'S RESTAURANT WAS DYING. A GRANDDAUGHTER POSTED ONE VIDEO WITHOUT TELLING THE OWNER. BY NOON THE PARKING LOT WAS FULL.**
- **What happened:** La Palma, a family Mexican restaurant near Ballard in Seattle, has run for 50 years and was facing closure. On Friday 30 October 2026 granddaughter Loren Jewett posted a TikTok and Instagram video asking for help, and promised to post every day for 30 days to see if social media could save it. It went viral overnight. The next day the restaurant was packed and the parking lot was full at noon.
- **The twist:** Owner Pedro Perez Estrella, 80, had no idea the posts existed. He found out when an employee called to say the restaurant was overflowing.
- **Why a business owner cares:** One honest video did what 50 years of word of mouth couldn't, and Jewett has now committed to posting daily. That daily posting is the part DropToPost automates.
- **Result:** **Reported** (KOMO): a packed house within a day and a busy weekend · 26,000+ likes, thousands of shares by the weekend. Revenue unknown. Whether it saves the restaurant is still open; the 30 days end around 29 Nov.
- **Dates:** Post 30 Oct 2026 · rush 31 Oct – 1 Nov 2026.
- **Proof:** [KOMO News](https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-mexican-restaurant-la-palma-ballard-facing-closure-community-support-viral-post-tiktok-instagram-50-years-economy-businesses)
- **Note:** Only one outlet so far, so Evidence is Medium. Follow Jewett's daily posts for an update video at day 30.

### 2026-11-03-B-2 · Story 89 · This month · Evidence High
**A DELIVERY APP LET AI DRAW RESTAURANT MENUS. "TIGER PRAWNS" CAME OUT AS ACTUAL TIGER CUBS.**
- **What happened:** In October 2026 Just Eat trialled an AI tool that generated food photos for small independent restaurants without their own pictures. The AI took dish names literally. A "jacket potato" became a puffer jacket with a potato print, "tiger prawn" became tiger cubs in a bowl, and "soda bread" became bacon and eggs floating in a glass of soda. Screenshots went viral, and on 20 October Just Eat apologised, removed the images and paused the rollout.
- **The twist:** It's the second act of a summer-long fight. In July a new San Francisco café, Grind & Unwind, put AI food photos in its window. Locals mocked it online, then graffitied the storefront. The owners pulled the pictures and put real pastries in the window within days.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Customers now spot AI content instantly and punish it publicly. The restaurants on Just Eat never chose those images and still took the hit.
- **Result:** **Verified:** Just Eat apologised and paused the tool (company statement). Grind & Unwind: graffiti cleanup over $700 (reported). Sales impact unknown.
- **Dates:** Grind & Unwind 2–6 Jul 2026 · Just Eat apology 20 Oct 2026.
- **Proof:** [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/food/just-eat-apologizes-after-ai-turns-food-into-literal-potato-jackets-tiger-cubs-bacon-soda-3421330/) · [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/10/21/just-eat-food-delivery-app-apologizes-for-ai-generated-menu-photo-showing-bacon-floating-in-soda/) · [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/dont-just-eat-slop-eat-ai-slop-just-eat-pauses-roll-out-of-generative-ai-photos-after-some-hilarious-results-but-they-still-seem-keen-to-eliminate-real-food-photography) · [NBC Bay Area (Grind & Unwind)](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/restaurant-backlash-ai-menu-san-francisco/4115961/)

### 2026-11-03-B-5 · Story 84 · Last 90 days · Evidence High
**AN EMPLOYEE ASKED AI TO WRITE A SIMPLE EMAIL SCRIPT. IT SHOWED 95,364 CUSTOMERS EACH OTHER'S EMAIL ADDRESSES.**
- **What happened:** A marketing employee at Bee Cheng Hiang, a well-known Singapore food brand, wanted to email members in batches. They asked an AI tool for a script that sends emails 1,000 at a time. It worked, but nobody had asked it to hide the recipients, so every email showed up to 999 other customers' addresses. The same person wrote and tested the code alone, and never checked what the test email actually showed.
- **The twist:** Singapore's privacy regulator called it the country's first reported AI-related data breach, and then said the AI hadn't malfunctioned. The AI did exactly what it was told. A person shipped its code without a second pair of eyes.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Automation saves hours, and one unchecked AI shortcut can put 95,000 customers' data in front of strangers.
- **Result:** **Verified:** 95,364 people affected (PDPC). The company now requires two staff to check every bulk email, and an independent review of any AI-written code that touches personal data.
- **Dates:** Incident 25 Apr 2026 · regulator decision published 30 Sep 2026.
- **Proof:** [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/10/bee-cheng-hiang-members-data-breach-ai/) · [eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/news-singapore-ai-data-breach-95000-emails-apac/) · [OECD AI Incidents](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2026-09-30-1509)
