### 2026-11-03-B-8 · Story 90 · This month · Evidence High
**HIS FAMILY LOST THEIR HOMES IN THE MAUI FIRE AND COULDN'T AFFORD AN INFLUENCER. SO HE BECAME ONE, AND THE TACO SHOP SOLD OUT.**
- **What happened:** The Haro family's homes in Lahaina were destroyed in the 2023 wildfires. They reopened as Lahaina Tacos, a tiny lunch-only takeout in downtown Honolulu. It took a year to get 1,000 followers. In late September 2026 owner Pedro Haro, 46, started posting videos of himself playing the influencers the family "can't afford to pay": in a cowboy hat, in a bathrobe doing a "Get Ready With Me" with pico de gallo as a face peel. In about three weeks the account went from ~1,000 to 37,000 followers, and the shop sold out and closed early one day.
- **The twist:** Haro calls himself "the shy one in the family". His day job is running the American Lung Association in Hawaii.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Zero budget, one phone, the owner's own face. "Before, if we almost broke even that day, it would be a good day."
- **Result:** **Self-reported** (TODAY): ~1,000 → 37,000 followers in ~3 weeks; one video 382,000+ likes; sold out one day; new merch line. **Self-reported** (Hawaii News Now): sales "at least doubled". Revenue unknown.
- **Dates:** Videos from late Sep 2026 · TODAY 13 Oct 2026 · HNN 16 Oct 2026.
- **Proof:** [TODAY](https://www.today.com/food/people/taco-shop-owner-influencer-video-lahaina-rcna603087) · [Hawaii News Now](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/10/16/viral-videos-help-boost-lahaina-tacos-sales/)

### 2026-11-03-B-9 · Story 88 · This month · Evidence High
**A POP STAR TOLD MILLIONS THAT A TINY BROOKLYN SHOP SNUBBED HER. THE SHOP'S ANSWER: THE BIRD IS NOW FOR SALE. $15 MILLION.**

- **Dates:** 10 Oct 2026 · price stunt 14 Oct 2026.
- **Proof:** [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/kelly-clarkson-bird-feud-brooklyn-shop-art-play-says-molly-now-sale-15-rcna603424) · [TODAY](https://www.today.com/popculture/kelly-clarkson-brooklyn-shop-video-owner-speaks-out-rcna603425) · [CBS New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/kelly-clarkson-dispute-brooklyn-store-surveillance-video/)

### 2026-11-03-B-10 · Story 82 · Breaking · Evidence Medium
**A "CUSTOMER" FOUND A COCKROACH IN THEIR DINNER AND WANTED S$300. THE OWNER RECOGNISED THE PHOTO: IT WAS FROM HIS OWN GOOGLE REVIEWS.**
- **What happened:** At 8 a.m. on 31 Oct 2026, Kok Sen Restaurant in Singapore's Chinatown got a WhatsApp complaint about a cockroach in a dish, with a demand for S$300 on a bill of about S$110. Owner Huang Zhaoqiang, 50, apologised and promised to investigate. The sender refused to wait and threatened to go online and to the authorities. Then Huang noticed the photo matched a recent Google review of his restaurant, with a cockroach seemingly added in. The receipt was from 22 Oct, not the day claimed.
- **The twist:** The scammer used disappearing messages and a foreign number, and the "evidence" came from the restaurant's own public reviews.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Every photo of your business online can now be edited into a complaint against you. Huang went public to warn other restaurants.
- **Result:** **Reported:** no money paid; police report filed. Nothing more yet.
- **Dates:** 31 Oct 2026 · reported 2 Nov 2026.
- **Proof:** [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/11/kok-sen-restaurant-cockroach/) (citing Shin Min Daily News)
- **Note:** One outlet, so Evidence Medium. Similar case the same month: a Serangoon stall's diner wanted S$5,000 for a cockroach; inspectors found no pests ([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/10/cockroach-char-kway-teow-compensation-deny-allegations/)).
