### 2026-11-26-B-1 · Candidate 7.5/10 · Recent (13 → 22 Nov 2026) · Evidence Medium
**A DELIVERY RIDER TASTED A CUSTOMER'S FRIED CHICKEN, DIDN'T LIKE IT, AND BOUGHT THE CUSTOMER A BETTER ONE. NINE DAYS LATER THE SHOP THAT MADE IT HAD TO SHUT ITS DOORS.**
- **What happened:** In Xuchang, Henan, a delivery rider filmed himself being offered a bite of a customer's fried chicken; he thought it was bad and bought the customer a portion he liked from a small pizza shop, calling it "treetree的" (a pun on 脆脆的, "crunchy"). The clip took off from 13 Nov. The pizza shop, run by the owner and his wife since 2019, normally sold 10–20 portions of fried chicken a day; now a day's batch sold out three minutes after going on the menu.
- **The turn:** The couple prepped until 1–2 a.m. for seven or eight nights and slept about four hours. Customers chased orders before dawn; the owner was filmed apologising at the door: "Sorry, I can't keep up." A seven-year-old pizza shop had been turned into a fried-chicken shop by someone who doesn't work there.
- **Payoff:** On 22 Nov the owner posted a video asking people to calm down and let him sleep before midnight, put up "sold out today" and closed to rest. He says he won't expand or hire, and will go back to pizza. The rider gained about 1.09M followers and had to deny it was a paid ad. The Paper headlines say he paid compensation out of his own pocket (details not confirmed).
- **Why anyone would watch:** A stranger's one-word review ("treetree!") nearly broke a two-person shop.
- **Why a business owner cares:** You don't choose what goes viral, or which product. Attention arrives faster than your kitchen can cope.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 8 · Narrative 7 · Business 9 · Evidence 7 · Freshness 10
- **How it unfolds:** (1) Rider dislikes a customer's chicken, buys a "treetree" one from a pizza shop. (2) Clip spreads; rider gains 1M+ followers. (3) Pizza shop's side dish sells out in three minutes a day. (4) Couple works to 2 a.m., apologises at the door before dawn. (5) Owner begs for rest and closes; "it's an ad" accusations.
- **Result:** Reported via The Paper, Sina (九派新闻, 大河报 reposts): a day's fried-chicken stock sold in 3 minutes; usual volume 10–20 portions/day (owner); shop paused trading on 22 Nov. Revenue: unknown. Rider's follower gain 1.087M (reported).

### 2026-11-26-B-2 · Candidate 7/10 · Last 90 days (16 Oct 2026) · Evidence Medium
**A CUSTOMER JUMPED THE QUEUE AND INSULTED THE DOUGHNUT GUY. HE VAULTED THE COUNTER AND CHASED HER DOWN THE STREET. THE NEXT DAY THE QUEUE WAS LONGER.**
- **What happened:** At 捲髮奶奶 (Curly Grandma) milk doughnuts in Ximending, Taipei, a woman tried to cut the queue on 16 Oct, was told to wait, then insulted the male clerk while ordering. They threw things at each other, and he jumped over the counter and chased her. Passers-by filmed it.
- **The turn:** Instead of a boycott, the clip (reported at 10M+ views) turned the clerk into a folk hero. People queued for days to buy doughnuts "to support him", and online commenters begged the shop not to fire him.
- **Payoff:** The shop posted a statement: the customer didn't follow the queue rules and insulted staff; throwing things was wrong; the clerk gets "internal coaching", not dismissal, and the shop apologised to frightened customers and promised better queue management. Police in Wanhua handled both under the Social Order Maintenance Act; neither pressed assault charges.
- **Why anyone would watch:** Everyone has wanted to do what he did, once.
- **Why a business owner cares:** A staff meltdown on camera can sell out your shop, and how you word the statement decides which way it goes.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 8 · Narrative 6 · Business 7 · Evidence 7 · Freshness 6
- **How it unfolds:** (1) Queue-jumper insults clerk. (2) Clerk vaults the counter, chases her, filmed. (3) 10M+ views; crowds come to back the clerk. (4) Shop keeps him, apologises carefully.
- **Result:** Reported via LTN, NOWnews: queues for days after the clip; sales figures unknown. The "10 million views" figure is from press, not read directly.
