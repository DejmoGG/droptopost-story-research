### 2026-11-12-B-1 · Pick 8.5/10 · Breaking (10–11 Nov, still unfolding) · Evidence High
**SHOP OWNERS ALL OVER CHINA STARTED HOLDING THE SAME RED BANNER: "BAN FOOD DELIVERY, SHUT DOWN LIVESTREAMS." STATE TV FOUND THEY WERE PAYING A MARKETING FIRM ¥3,800 A MONTH TO SAY IT.**
- **What happened:** For weeks, short videos showed owners of small physical shops in China holding a red banner reading "取消外卖 关闭直播 恢复实体繁荣" ("cancel delivery, shut down livestreams, bring back real shops"), riding anger that apps and live-selling are killing high-street stores. On 10 Nov CCTV traced the wave to Sichuan Shizhou Culture Media, a Yibin company registered in March 2026 with ¥100,000 of capital, running a brand called "Sichuan Tongzhou Hui" (四川同舟会). Its operator, known online as "Binlang Ge" (槟榔哥), sold shops account management, reportedly ¥3,800 a month for 15 videos; the banner was the hook.
- **The turn:** The protesters weren't protesting. A banquet restaurant owner in Yibin told reporters he had never offered delivery and that the video idea came from the agency; another shop in the series still did delivery; one was a clothing shop that had nothing to do with food. The operator admitted the banner was a tested template, made deliberately low-quality ("没质感是故意的"), for "violent account growth" (暴力起号): "nobody likes or shares our stuff, so we use methods."
- **Payoff:** On 11 Nov Yibin's cyberspace office, police and market regulator formed a joint investigation team, ordered the company to stop, and opened an investigation into the people in charge; the operator's account went private. Copycat banner videos were still appearing on new templates.
- **Why anyone would watch:** A grassroots protest against the internet turned out to be an internet growth hack, and the "restaurant" was a clothing shop.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Shops paid for outrage content to get attention, and the attention they got was a police investigation.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 8 · Narrative 9 · Business 9 · Evidence 8 · Freshness 10
- **How it unfolds:** (1) Identical red-banner videos spread: small owners "begging" to ban delivery and livestreams. (2) Comment sections fill with sympathy and arguments. (3) CCTV reporters visit the shops: no delivery to cancel, or delivery still running, or no food at all. (4) The agency boss admits on camera it's a template to grow accounts. (5) Police and regulators open a joint investigation the next day; the account goes dark.
- **Result:** **Verified** (CCTV via The Paper, CNR, Guangming, China Economic Net, Jiemian, Sina Finance): joint investigation team formed 11 Nov; company ordered to stop; investigation of responsible persons under way. Fee (¥3,800/month for 15 videos) **reported** (SMZDM, citing the agency's pitch). Penalties: unknown yet.

Seoul's Jung-gu district plans to add the market to the government's crowd-management system.
