**A REPAIR-SHOP OWNER POSTED A VIDEO ABOUT HIS FEUD WITH THE PIZZERIA NEXT DOOR. THE INTERNET SENT THE DEATH THREATS TO A DIFFERENT PIZZERIA AN HOUR AWAY.**
- **What happened:** Leo Mastrolia, 25, who runs Digital Doctor Repairs in Manahawkin, New Jersey, posted TikTok footage of a police officer telling him the owner of Nick's Pizza & Pasta in the same strip mall had filed a harassment complaint against him. The clip reached 3.1M views in under a day (Dexerto; the NY Post put it at 4.5M). Viewers went after "Nick's Pizza", but many picked the wrong one: Nick's Pizza and Pasta in Cinnaminson, more than an hour away and unrelated.
- **The turn:** The Cinnaminson owners, Nick Lubrano and Sonia Scotto, got 60+ one-star reviews in a single day, suspicious out-of-state orders, and calls including "I'm gonna kill your family." They only learned why when a stranger spotted the mix-up and tracked them down.
- **Payoff:** Mastrolia drove to Cinnaminson, apologised in person, filmed a video redirecting his followers, and handed the couple **$1,000** to give away as free pizza. The man who started the pile-on ended up paying for the wrong pizzeria's promotion. Whether the threats have stopped: unknown.
- **Why anyone would watch:** An internet mob with the wrong address, and the man who started it showing up with cash.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Your shop's name is shared by hundreds of others. Someone else's viral feud can land on your Google page overnight.

### 2026-11-25-B-3 · Candidate 7/10 · This month (late Oct → 6 Nov 2026) · Evidence High
**A FAMOUS COMEDIAN PRAISED A TINY PIE SHOP ON NATIONAL TV. THE OWNER PUT A SIGN ON HIS DOOR: "THE PIES ARE VERY ORDINARY. HE WAS JUST HUNGRY."**
- **What happened:** On the variety show 《现在就出发》, actor-comedian Shen Teng raved about a northeastern meat-pie shop in Danzhou, Sanya (立民馅饼, owner Wang Limin), saying you can hardly get pies this good even in the northeast. Queues of 30–40 people formed at peak hours; one report clocked 70 minutes.
- **The turn:** Wang posted a sign at the door: "These pies are very ordinary, Shen Teng was just too hungry", telling people to buy less and eat them hot. He said he feared people would come with impossible expectations and be let down. The sign became the thing people queued to photograph. "The more he discouraged, the more people wanted to go."
- **Payoff:** Wang says daily takings at his two shops have **doubled** and he won't raise prices (4–7 yuan, unchanged for 6–7 years). Backstory: Shen Teng is a six-year regular; the first time, Wang reopened his closed shop after 9 p.m. because a customer called to say Shen wanted pies.
- **Why anyone would watch:** The owner tried to talk people out of his own food, and it made them queue longer.
- **Why a business owner cares:** Honest anti-hype can sell harder than hype, as long as the product holds up.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 7 · Narrative 6 · Business 8 · Evidence 8 · Freshness 8
- **How it unfolds:** (1) Celebrity praises the shop on TV. (2) Queues. (3) Owner's "very ordinary" sign. (4) Sign becomes the attraction; takings double.
- **Result:** Self-reported (owner, via Hongcan/ChinaVenture and The Paper): daily revenue doubled at two shops. Prices unchanged.
