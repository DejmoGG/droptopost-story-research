### 2026-11-18-B-1 · Candidate 7.5/10 · This month (4–12 Nov) · Evidence Medium
**A RESTAURANT'S VIDEO OF A MOTHER EATING SEVEN BOWLS OF PLAIN RICE, AND THE KIND OWNER WHO SECRETLY SLIPPED HER ¥300, MADE CHINA CRY. THE OWNER HAD STAGED THE WHOLE THING.**
- **What happened:** Around 4 Nov a restaurant in Liangping, Chongqing posted a short video: a mother and daughter order only a ¥6 plate of stir-fried bean sprouts, the mother gives it to the child and eats seven bowls of plain rice, and the owner, "Sister Ying" (英姐), quietly adds dishes to their takeaway bag with a note and ¥300 in cash. It spread across Chinese platforms as a feel-good clip.
- **The turn:** Viewers spotted holes: why would someone that poor pick a sit-down restaurant, does the ¥6 dish exist, and who filmed it? Others found that the owner had posted a tearful video about her struggling business in May and had long used short videos to promote the shop. A store manager told reporters the video was the shop's own online promotion; one report says the dish had sold only twice in the shop's system.
- **Payoff:** On 12 Nov Liangping's joint investigation team announced the video was staged: the restaurant's 57-year-old operator, surnamed Li, invented the story and organised people to act it out "to gain attention and traffic". A case was opened against her for misleading the public and disturbing online order. Penalty unknown.
- **Why anyone would watch:** The kindest person in the video was the only one acting.
- **Why a business owner cares:** A struggling owner faked a good deed to get customers, and the faked kindness is now an official case with her name on it.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 8 · Narrative 7 · Business 7 · Evidence 7 · Freshness 9

(3) Reporters visit; the owner refuses to talk, a manager admits it was promotion. (4) Officials declare it staged and open a case against the operator.
- **Result:** Verified (Liangping joint investigation team notice, via The Paper, Huxiu, China.com, Sina): case opened 12 Nov 2026. Effect on sales: unknown. "Sold twice" figure: reported once, unconfirmed.
