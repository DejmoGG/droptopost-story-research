## B1. VIRAL MARKETING MIRACLES & DISASTERS

### B1-1 · Viral 92 · Evidence High · Verified
**A KOREAN CREATOR KEPT CALLING IT "BASELINE", SO VASELINE RENAMED ITSELF**
- **Hook:** A beauty creator mispronounced the brand on camera, and instead of correcting her, Vaseline changed its name to match.
- **What happened:** Korean skincare creator Min Day Lee kept saying "Baseline" in her videos (Korean adapts the English "V" sound). One clip went massively viral. In late August 2026 Vaseline played along: its UK Instagram posted a mock "official statement" renaming the brand, switched to a "Baseline" logo, reposted her clip, and named her "CEO of Baseline, no interview needed". Its India account joined in.
- **Biggest twist:** A global brand let a creator's mistake overrule its own name, for a while.
- **Why it could go viral:** Funny, harmless, and anyone can join the joke.
- **Why our audience cares:** Following the creator is cheaper and faster than producing your own ad. Brands that play along with a meme get the attention for free.
- **Financial results:** unknown (temporary social stunt, not a legal rename).
- **Audience signals:** Reported views range from **~2M (Famous Campaigns) to 36M (Express Tribune)**. Not checked on-platform.
- **Original visuals:** Min Day Lee's videos and Vaseline UK's Instagram "statement" (linked in coverage).
- **Dates:** viral clip Aug 2026 · brand response late Aug 2026 · coverage to Oct 2026.
- **Sources:** [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2026/09/03/vaseline-has-renamed-themselves-in-response-to-a-massively-viral-video/) · [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2626058/vaseline-renames-itself-baseline-after-korean-creators-viral-pronunciation-racks-up-36m-views) · [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/vaseline-actually-changes-name-after-viral-baseline-pronunciation-3402876/) · [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/how-a-korean-skincare-influencer-accidentally-made-vaseline-change-its-name-to-baseline/3027174/)

### B1-2 · Viral 89 · Evidence High · Verified (sales lift is company-reported)
**"SYDNEY SWEENEY HAS GREAT JEANS": THE AD THE INTERNET HATED, THEN THE STOCK JUMPED**
- **Hook:** A one-line pun on "genes" sparked a culture war. Six weeks later the retailer said it was its best customer acquisition ever.
- **What happened:** In July 2025 American Eagle launched a denim campaign with actor Sydney Sweeney built on the line "great jeans". Critics called it a nod to eugenics, and it became a political fight that even drew comments from President Trump. The brand said it "is and always was about the jeans". In September 2025 it reported record new-customer acquisition, and the stock jumped. In October 2026 the CMO told The Drum how the first days felt: a commercial win, then cancelled on TikTok.
- **Biggest twist:** The backlash didn't hurt sales. The Sydney Jean reportedly sold out in a week.
- **Why it could go viral:** Celebrity, controversy, and a clear money ending.
- **Why our audience cares:** Shows how a polarising hook can pay, and why it's a bet, not a strategy.
- **Financial results:** Q3 revenue **+6%** (company). Q4 comparable-sales outlook raised to **+8–9%** (company). Stock **+~20–25% after hours** on earnings (CNN, Fox). "Record" new customers (company claim).
- **Audience signals:** not measured.
- **Original visuals:** the American Eagle "Great Jeans" spots. The creative also ran on the Las Vegas Sphere.
- **Dates:** launch Jul 2025 · earnings 3 Sep 2025 · CMO interview 20 Oct 2026.
- **Sources:** [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/tv/news/american-eagle-sydney-sweeney-great-jeans-sales-boost-1236506947) · [CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2025/09/03/american-eagle-stock-surges-25-after-sydney-sweeneys-good-jeans-ad-campaign-boosts-brand/) · [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/you-ve-been-canceled-on-tiktok-american-eagle-s-cmo-on-the-sydney-sweeney-backlash)

## B2. AI & CONTENT PRODUCTION

### B2-1 · Viral 90 · Evidence High · Verified
**TWO PAGES IN VOGUE, AND THE MODEL WASN'T A PERSON**
- **Hook:** Readers flipped through the August issue of Vogue and found a Guess ad where the model was made by AI, flagged only in tiny print.
- **What happened:** Guess ran a two-page ad in the August 2025 US Vogue print issue with AI-generated models, made by London agency Seraphinne Vallora. The disclosure was one small line ("Produced by Seraphinne Vallora on AI"). Models and readers were furious ("lazy and cheap", said model Felicity Hayward), and Vogue said it was a paid ad, not editorial. The founders say Guess co-founder Paul Marciano approached them directly.
- **Biggest twist:** The agency was two young founders, not a studio. They say they still hire photographers.
- **Why it could go viral:** Vogue is the symbol of human fashion work, and most readers didn't notice.
- **Why our audience cares:** AI content can now reach the most prestigious placements. The backlash comes from hiding it, not from the tool.
- **Financial results:** unknown.
- **Audience signals:** not measured.
- **Original visuals:** the Guess Vogue spread (reproduced in coverage) and the agency's Instagram.
- **Dates:** Aug 2025 issue (late July 2025).

### B2-4 · Viral 84 · Evidence High · Verified
**AN AUTOMATED VIDEO CAMPAIGN PUT INFAMOUS CRIMINALS IN GARY LINEKER'S HANDS**
- **Hook:** Walkers let fans' selfies drop automatically into personalised videos of Gary Lineker. Trolls sent in photos of notorious criminals.
- **What happened:** In May 2017 Walkers ran #WalkersWave to give away Champions League final tickets. Fans tweeted a selfie and a bot posted a video of Lineker holding their photo and saying thanks. Users submitted photos of Jimmy Savile, Rolf Harris and others, and the bot published them. Walkers shut it down that evening, deleted the tweets and apologised. Users had already saved copies.
- **Biggest twist:** Nobody hacked anything. The automation did exactly what it was built to do.
- **Why it could go viral:** Automated personalised video is everywhere again with AI, and this is the classic warning.
- **Why our audience cares:** Every automated content tool needs a human or a moderation filter before anything goes public.
- **Financial results:** unknown.
- **Audience signals:** not measured.
- **Original visuals:** preserved screenshots and re-uploads (in coverage).
- **Dates:** 25 May 2017.

## B3. SMALL BUSINESSES WINNING THROUGH CONTENT

### B3-1 · Viral 87 · Evidence High · Verified
**ONE TIKTOK: FROM ONE BATCH OF DOUGH EVERY TWO WEEKS TO SIX OR SEVEN A DAY**
- **Hook:** A 21-year-old's introduction video was watched 16 million times, and the line now wraps around the shop.
- **What happened:** Laura Molinar started Pan Pan, a Mexican-Japanese fusion bakery (milk-bread conchas, horchata matcha), with pop-ups in high school and opened a shop at 20 in Dalworthington Gardens, Texas. Her TikTok introducing herself and the shop went viral. Hour-long lines followed, even on weekday afternoons. She now talks about more stores and franchising.
- **Biggest twist:** One self-introduction video did it, with no ad and no influencer.
- **Why it could go viral:** A clear before/after (dough batches) and a young founder.
- **Why our audience cares:** A founder showing their face and story is the cheapest marketing a local business has.
- **Financial results:** unknown. Production went from **1 dough every two weeks → 6–7 a day** (her mother, NBC).
- **Audience signals:** **16M+ views** (NBC, her figure) / **17M+** (The Shorthorn). Not checked on-platform.
- **Original visuals:** Pan Pan's TikTok introduction video.
- **Dates:** viral video early August (coverage 2024–2025; exact year not confirmed in sources found) · latest coverage 2025.
- **Sources:** [NBC DFW](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/local/pan-pan-bakery-tiktok-star-owner/3904212/) · [The Shorthorn](https://www.theshorthorn.com/life_and_entertainment/food_and_drink/pan-demonium-in-arlington-area-bakery-draws-hour-long-lines-for-mexican-japanese-treats/article_ebb9b6d4-e0ac-48e2-909c-1d37dc3dbdc0.html) · [Texas Standard](https://www.texasstandard.org/stories/panpan-bakery-cafe-arlington-tx-laura-molinar/)

### B3-2 · Viral 84 · Evidence Medium · Partially verified (figures via Inc., owner)
**SHE ANSWERED MS. RACHEL'S POST AND PAID A MONTH'S RENT IN ONE DAY**
- **Hook:** A struggling LA cookie maker replied to one post on Threads, and the next morning Ms. Rachel reposted her.
- **What happened:** Ashley La Bomme-Benson runs The Bomme Sweets, a custom-cookie bakery in Los Angeles. Custom orders had brought in only about $7,000 in 2026 as customers cut back. On 11 August 2026 children's star Ms. Rachel asked Black entrepreneurs on Threads to share their businesses. She replied, and Ms. Rachel reposted and followed her. Ten orders came in within ~10 minutes.

- **Why it could go viral:** Timely, kind, and a hard number.
- **Why our audience cares:** A reply in the right thread can be worth more than a month of posting. Showing up where big accounts invite you matters.
- **Financial results:** ~$7K custom orders in 2026 before (owner) · day one covered **$3,650** rent · **102 orders in two weeks** (Inc., via outlets).
- **Audience signals:** **330,000+ views on Threads** (reported).
- **Original visuals:** Ms. Rachel's 11 Aug Threads post and repost.
- **Dates:** 11–12 Aug 2026 · reported early Sep 2026.
