# The owner's taste (read before every Report B run)

The owner's own verdicts outrank the brief's wording. Add a line for every verdict: newest first, never delete.

## Loved
- **KitKat: 413,793 bars stolen, then KitKat asked the internet to help find them** (2026-11-03-B1-1). A real crime, an absurd number, a mystery, a fresh 2026 event, entertainment first. The business response is part of the story, not a lesson bolted on.

## Rejected, and why
- **The rest of the 2026-11-03 Report B (26 stories): "totally boring", "all old".** They were successful marketing case studies (Popeyes, KFC, Ocean Spray, Dollar Shave Club, Blendtec, Poppi…) dressed up as stories. Too old, too well known, and rewarded for business results instead of an unbelievable event.
- **The North Face helicopter jacket:** "a clever advertisement, barely any real conflict or suspense".
- **Viral-but-useless stories** (a bank's sheep mascot, a Pringles burial urn, a wrong costume delivered): entertaining, but nothing a business owner can use. They attract the wrong audience.

## What the owner wants (the owner's words, condensed)
- "Stories that make me say: what the fuck, that actually happened?"
- "Extraordinary true stories about ordinary businesses that used clever, inexpensive or automated content to achieve unbelievable commercial results."
- Fresh first: the last 7, 30 and 90 days. Old stories only if exceptionally strong and unfamiliar.
- Actually research Reddit, Google Trends, news and viral posts. Never claim interest without evidence.
- No filler, no repeats.
