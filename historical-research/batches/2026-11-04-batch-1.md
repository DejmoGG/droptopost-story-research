### 2026-11-04-H-01 · 8.5/10 · 2010 · Netherlands / South Africa · Beer · Evidence High
**36 WOMEN IN IDENTICAL ORANGE DRESSES WERE THROWN OUT OF A WORLD CUP MATCH, AND TWO WERE ARRESTED. THE DRESS CAME FREE WITH A PACK OF BEER.**
- **Story:** Dutch brewer Bavaria had been giving away a short orange "Dutchy Dress" with eight-packs of its beer since April 2010. On 14 June 2010, about 36 women wore it to Netherlands v Denmark at Soccer City, Johannesburg. FIFA's officials ejected them for ambush marketing, because Budweiser was the tournament's official beer. South African police arrested two Dutch women, Barbara Castelein and Mirte Nieuwpoort, under a law passed for the World Cup that made ambush marketing a crime.
- **Most surprising part:** A short orange dress was enough to get two women arrested under a marketing law.
- **What happened afterward:** The Dutch government called the arrests disproportionate, the charges were dropped about a week later, and the story ran worldwide. One South African estimate put Bavaria's free publicity there at R750,000 or more, and the Amsterdam Historical Museum took one of the dresses into its collection. Bavaria denied organising anything; FIFA said it had flown in two coordinators.
- **Year:** 2010
- **Original material:** Agency photos of the women in the stands and being escorted out (Reuters/AP, widely published). Reuters TV clip: Dutch brewer defends its World Cup marketing (reuters.screenocean.com, linked). Original match TV footage not checked.
- **Sources:** [Mail & Guardian: two Dutch women arrested](https://mg.co.za/article/2010-06-16-two-dutch-women-arrested-over-ambush-marketing) · [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2010/06/16/little-orange-dresses-ruffle-feathers-at-the-world-cup/) · [TimesLive: 'Orange dress' campaign worked](https://www.timeslive.co.za/sunday-times/business/2010-06-29-orange-dress-campaign-worked/) · [TimesLive: dress joins museum](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/world/2010-07-15-bavaria-mini-dress-joins-museum/) · [Reuters video record](https://reuters.screenocean.com/record/783231)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** Thirty-six women in matching dresses at a World Cup match, and police treating a dress as a crime.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** The biggest sponsor paid for the tournament; a smaller brewer got the headlines with a free dress. It also shows how far a rights holder will go to protect a sponsorship.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 9 · Evidence 9
- **Familiarity:** Known in sports-marketing circles; rarely told as a story.
- **Careful:** Who organised it is disputed (FIFA says Bavaria; Bavaria denied it). Head counts vary from 30 to 40.

### 2026-11-04-H-02 · 8.5/10 · 2001 · USA · Restaurants · Evidence High
**A HOOTERS WAITRESS WON A BEER-SALES CONTEST FOR "A NEW TOYOTA". BLINDFOLDED IN THE CAR PARK, SHE WAS HANDED A TOY YODA.**
- **Story:** In 2001 the Hooters in Panama City Beach, Florida ran a beer-sales contest for its waitresses, and Jodee Berry understood the prize to be a new Toyota. She won. Blindfolded, she was led to the car park, where the blindfold came off to reveal a toy Yoda, the Star Wars doll. The manager said it was an April Fool's joke. She quit and sued the franchise owner, Gulf Coast Wings Inc., for breach of contract and fraudulent misrepresentation.
- **Most surprising part:** A pun was the whole prize: "Toyota" said fast is "toy Yoda".
- **What happened afterward:** It settled in May 2002 on undisclosed terms. Her lawyer said she could go to a local dealer and "pick out whatever type of Toyota she wants". The case still turns up in law-school contract examples.
- **Year:** 2001
- **Original material:** No video. Court filings and AP coverage; photos of Berry with the toy were published by newspapers at the time (not checked).
- **Sources:** [HeraldNet (AP): settles 'toy Yoda' lawsuit](https://heraldnet.com/news/former-hooters-waitress-settles-toy-yoda-lawsuit) · [Tampa Bay Times, 2001](https://www.tampabay.com/archive/2001/07/28/dream-car-is-a-toy-yoda) · [Deseret News, 2001](https://www.deseret.com/2001/7/29/19598705/oh-what-a-sour-feeling-toy-yoda) · [Missouri Lawyers Media](https://molawyersmedia.com/2002/06/10/toying-with-the-law/)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** A blindfold, a car park and a pun. The ending (she got a car after all) lands in one line.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** A staff contest is a promise. Joke the prize and you've written a contract you'll pay for.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 7 · Evidence 8
- **Familiarity:** A known oddity (Snopes, law blogs); unfamiliar to most viewers outside the US.
- **Careful:** Settlement terms were never disclosed. One blog says she got a Toyota; the AP account only quotes her lawyer, and that is the version to use.

### 2026-11-04-H-03 · 8.5/10 · 2012 · USA · Retail / music · Evidence High
**WALMART LET FACEBOOK PICK WHICH STORE THE RAPPER PITBULL WOULD VISIT. THE INTERNET VOTED TO SEND HIM TO AN ISLAND IN ALASKA.**
- **Story:** In summer 2012, Walmart and Sheets Energy Strips promised that Pitbull would make an appearance at whichever Walmart store got the most new Facebook likes. Writers at the comedy site Something Awful, led by David Thorpe, started #ExilePitbull to send him to the most remote Walmart in the US, in Kodiak, Alaska. Kodiak had more than 70,000 likes when voting closed on 16 July and won by a mile.
- **Most surprising part:** Pitbull didn't fight it. He said he'd go, and he went.
- **What happened afterward:** He visited Kodiak in late July 2012, was given bear repellent by locals, and the coverage was overwhelmingly positive. A prank meant to humiliate him became the campaign's best publicity.
- **Year:** 2012
- **Original material:** TV news coverage of the Kodiak visit and his own video reply exist (ABC, Alaska outlets; not checked this pass). Photos of the visit in ADN and ABC News.
- **Sources:** [Anchorage Daily News: Pitbull faces exile](https://www.adn.com/alaska-beat/article/rapper-pitbull-faces-exile-alaska-island-if-facebook-fans-have-their-way/2012/06/30/) · [ADN: David Thorpe on why](https://www.adn.com/alaska-beat/article/david-thorpe-why-hes-trying-exile-pitbull-kodiak/2012/07/04) · [Adweek: it's official](https://www.adweek.com/?p=410850) · [ABC News: Kodiak and bear repellent](https://abcnews.go.com/blogs/entertainment/2012/08/rapper-pitbull-visits-kodiak-alaska-and-gets-bear-repellent/)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** The internet tries to banish a pop star to the end of the earth and he turns up smiling.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** Open votes get hijacked. The winning move is to play along, which turned the joke into goodwill.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 8 · Evidence 9
- **Familiarity:** Known to people online in 2012; mostly forgotten.
- **Careful:** Sources credit Thorpe alone or with Something Awful's John Hendren.

### 2026-11-04-H-06 · 8.5/10 · 2006–2007 · USA · Beverages · Evidence High
**A COCA-COLA SECRETARY OFFERED COKE'S SECRETS TO PEPSI FOR $1.5 MILLION. PEPSI CALLED COCA-COLA.**
- **Story:** In May 2006 PepsiCo received a letter offering Coca-Cola trade secrets, including samples of a new product, to the highest bidder. Pepsi forwarded it to Coca-Cola, and the FBI set up an undercover investigation. It led to Joya Williams, a secretary at Coca-Cola's Atlanta headquarters, and two accomplices, who asked for $1.5 million.
- **Most surprising part:** The rival who would gain most refused and helped catch the leak.
- **What happened afterward:** Williams was convicted in February 2007 and sentenced in May to 8 years in prison; co-conspirator Ibrahim Dimson got 5. The judge praised Pepsi's "good corporate citizenship". Coca-Cola confirmed the documents were genuine.
- **Year:** 2006–2007
- **Original material:** Court records (DOJ sentencing release archived at UNT) and TV news coverage of the trial. Undercover video was shown at trial (reported; not checked).
- **Sources:** [CNN Money: 2 sentenced](https://money.cnn.com/2007/05/23/news/newsmakers/coke/) · [CBS News: 8 years](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ex-coke-secretary-gets-8-years-in-jail/) · [DOJ sentencing release (archive)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090506152158/http:/www.cybercrime.gov/williamsSent.pdf) · [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2007/05/24/Prison-looms-for-Coca-Cola-recipe-thief/)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** The most famous rivalry in business and one side protects the other.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** Your biggest risk can sit at the desk outside your office, and your competitor may value its reputation more than your secrets.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 8 · Evidence 10
- **Familiarity:** Covered widely in 2006–07; unfamiliar to most viewers today.
- **Careful:** It was not the secret formula: it was a new-product sample and confidential documents. Don't say "the recipe".

### 2026-11-04-H-07 · 8.5/10 · 1992 · UK · Appliances · Evidence High
**HOOVER PROMISED TWO FREE FLIGHTS WITH ANY £100 VACUUM CLEANER. BRITAIN BOUGHT VACUUMS TO FLY TO AMERICA, AND HOOVER SOLD ITS EUROPEAN BUSINESS AT A LOSS.**
- **Story:** In late 1992 Hoover's UK division offered two free return flights to anyone who spent at least £100 on its products. The offer grew to include New York and Orlando, a package worth £600 or more. People bought vacuum cleaners they didn't need just to get the tickets, and Hoover couldn't supply the flights.
- **Most surprising part:** The prize was worth several times the product, so the customers were buying flights and getting a free vacuum.
- **What happened afterward:** Protests and court cases ran until 1998. Hoover fired the president of its UK division and two senior marketing executives. Cost estimates range from about £48 million to more than £100 million, and in 1995 Hoover Europe was sold to the Italian company Candy at a loss.
- **Year:** 1992
- **Original material:** BBC news reports and the original press ads are cited in Wikipedia's references (not opened this pass).
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia (with BBC/Guardian references)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_free_flights_promotion) · [The Hustle: the worst sales promotion in history](https://thehustle.co/the-worst-sales-promotion-in-history)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** Free flights to Florida with a vacuum cleaner. Everyone sees the problem in the first sentence.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** Always price the worst case: what happens if far more people take the offer than you planned for?
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 9 · Evidence 8
- **Familiarity:** Famous in UK marketing and business teaching. The owner may know it.
- **Careful:** Total cost varies by source (£48m, ~£50m, >£100m). Use "tens of millions of pounds".

### 2026-11-04-H-04 · 8.5/10 · 2004–2014 · North / South Korea · Snacks · Evidence Medium
**SOUTH KOREAN FACTORIES PAID NORTH KOREAN WORKERS BONUSES IN CHOCO PIES. THEY BECAME BLACK-MARKET MONEY, AND PYONGYANG BANNED THEM.**
- **Story:** At the Kaesong industrial park, where South Korean companies employed North Korean workers, cash bonuses weren't allowed, so firms handed out Choco Pies, the chocolate-coated marshmallow cakes. Workers resold them instead of eating them. Chosun Ilbo reported in 2010 that one could fetch as much as US$9.50 on the North Korean black market, and one estimate put Kaesong's consumption at about 2.5 million a month.
- **Most surprising part:** A snack cake became a currency and a symbol of the South, inside the North.
- **What happened afterward:** In mid-2014 the North told Kaesong firms to stop and offered sausages and other items instead. South Korean activists answered by floating 10,000 Choco Pies over the border on balloons, and the North later made its own knock-off.
- **Year:** 2004–2014
- **Original material:** No original footage. Visuals: the product, Kaesong photos, the balloon launches (photographed by agencies, not checked).
- **Sources:** [Quartz: North Korea cracks down](https://qz.com/228067/north-korea-is-cracking-down-on-a-chocolate-covered-marshmallow-filled-black-market-currency) · [Washington Times, 2 Jul 2014](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2014/jul/2/north-korea-bans-choco-pies-as-factory-pay-says-us/) · [VOA: N. Korea cuts Choco Pie supply](https://www.voanews.com/a/no-sweet-surrender-n-korea-cuts-choco-pie-supply/1940068.html) · [NK Econ Watch (collected reports)](https://www.nkeconwatch.com/2015/07/14/south-koreas-subversive-chocko-pies/)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** A cheap supermarket cake is worth ten dollars in the most closed country on earth.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** Your product is whatever customers turn it into. Here it became money, then a political threat.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 8 · Business 8 · Evidence 7
- **Familiarity:** Little known outside Korea.
- **Careful:** Black-market prices and volumes are press estimates, not official figures. Some sources date the ban to 2012; most say 2014.

### 2026-11-04-H-09 · 8.5/10 · 1991 · UK · Jewellery retail · Evidence High
**A JEWELLERY BOSS TOLD A ROOM OF DIRECTORS THAT HIS PRODUCTS WERE "TOTAL CRAP". THE JOKE WIPED ABOUT £500 MILLION OFF HIS COMPANY.**
- **Story:** On 23 April 1991 Gerald Ratner, who ran Britain's biggest jewellery chain, spoke to the Institute of Directors at the Royal Albert Hall. He joked that some of his products were "total crap" and that a pair of earrings was cheaper than a Marks & Spencer prawn sandwich and probably wouldn't last as long. The press made it national news.
- **Most surprising part:** It was a throwaway joke he had used before, to a room that wasn't his customers.
- **What happened afterward:** Ratners Group lost about £500 million in value, shops closed, and Ratner left the company (sources give 1992–93). "Doing a Ratner" became business slang for ruining your own product with your own words.
- **Year:** 1991
- **Original material:** Clips of the speech and later interviews with Ratner exist on TV and YouTube (not checked this pass).
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Gerald Ratner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ratner) · [Jewish News interview](https://www.jewishnews.co.uk/gerald-ratner-i-am-not-the-person-the-tabloids-portrayed/) · [The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/gerald-ratners-billion-dollar-speech)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** Ten seconds of a speech, half a billion pounds gone.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** Customers hear everything you say about them, even at a private dinner.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 8 · Narrative 9 · Business 10 · Evidence 9
- **Familiarity:** Very famous in the UK; standard business-school example. The owner may know it.
- **Careful:** The exact wording of the prawn-sandwich line varies. The £500m is a widely cited estimate, not an exact figure.

### 2026-11-04-H-08 · 8/10 · 1986 · USA · Nonprofit / events · Evidence Medium
**A CHARITY RELEASED 1.4 MILLION BALLOONS OVER CLEVELAND TO SET A WORLD RECORD. THEY CAME DOWN ON THE LAKE WHERE TWO FISHERMEN WERE MISSING.**
- **Story:** On 27 September 1986 the United Way of Cleveland released about 1.4 million balloons from Public Square to set a Guinness record and raise money. A storm front pushed them back down over the city and onto Lake Erie. The Coast Guard was searching the lake for two missing fishermen, and rescuers said the floating balloons hampered the search.
- **Most surprising part:** The record-breaking photo and the disaster are the same picture.
- **What happened afterward:** The two men's bodies washed ashore. One widow sued the United Way and the organiser for $3.2 million and settled on undisclosed terms; a horse owner whose Arabian horses were spooked by landing balloons also settled. The event had cost about $500,000 to stage.
- **Year:** 1986
- **Original material:** Press photos of the release are widely published (Wikipedia, Case Western encyclopedia). Local TV footage of the release exists (not checked this pass).
- **Sources:** [Encyclopedia of Cleveland History (Case Western)](https://case.edu/ech/articles/b/balloonfest) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloonfest_%2786)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** One of the most beautiful sights a city ever staged, and then it falls on a rescue.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** A stunt is judged by its worst outcome, not its best photo.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 7 · Evidence 7
- **Familiarity:** A known internet history oddity; not the owner's usual sources.
- **Careful:** Whether the balloons cost the fishermen their lives can't be proven; say only that rescuers said they hampered the search. Loss figures beyond the $500,000 are unconfirmed.

### 2026-11-04-H-11 · 8/10 · 1985 → 2009 → 2023 · Japan · Fast food / sport · Evidence High
**BASEBALL FANS THREW A COLONEL SANDERS STATUE INTO A RIVER. THEIR TEAM DIDN'T WIN THE JAPAN SERIES AGAIN FOR 38 YEARS.**
- **Story:** When the Hanshin Tigers won the 1985 Japan Series, celebrating fans in Osaka jumped into the Dotonbori canal, one for each player they resembled. Nobody looked like the American star Randy Bass, so they grabbed a life-size Colonel Sanders statue from a KFC and threw it in. Hanshin then went decades without a title, and fans blamed "the Curse of the Colonel".
- **Most surprising part:** A fast-food mascot became a national superstition.
- **What happened afterward:** Divers found the statue in the river in March 2009, missing its glasses and left hand. Hanshin won the Japan Series again in 2023, 38 years later. KFC's Japanese parent said in 2024 that the statue had been disposed of.
- **Year:** 1985 → 2009 → 2023
- **Original material:** Photos of the recovered statue (2009, AFP and others). 1985 celebration footage exists (not checked).
- **Sources:** [MLB.com](https://www.mlb.com/guardians/news/the-japanese-team-cursed-by-colonel-sanders) · [AFP (via eNCA): statue disposed of](https://www.enca.com/lifestyle/curse-colonel-kfc-statue-disposed-japan) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_the_Colonel)
- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** A curse, a river, a statue and a 38-year wait.
- **Why entrepreneurs would care:** A brand mascot can take on a life you never planned, for decades.
- **Scores:** Entertainment 9 · Narrative 9 · Business 6 · Evidence 8
- **Familiarity:** Known to baseball fans; fairly well known online.
- **Careful:** Accounts differ on how the statue was found (canal cleaning vs construction). Verify the 2023 Japan Series win and the 2024 disposal against the original reports before use.

- **Why ordinary viewers would care:** Ten-cent beer, 25,000 people and an obvious ending that still shocks.

- **Familiarity:** Famous to US sports fans.
