### A1-1 · Viral 95 · Evidence High · Verified (admission, probe, share falls) / Alleged (espionage claims)
**THE MOST VALUABLE CHIP COMPANY ON EARTH HELD AN EMERGENCY BOARD MEETING TO DECIDE WHETHER TO FIRE ITS FOUNDER. THE REASON: AN AFFAIR WITH A WOMAN US INTELLIGENCE REPORTEDLY CALLS A CHINESE SPY.**
- **Hook:** Jensen Huang built Nvidia over 33 years; one memo to staff put his job on the table in four days.
- **What happened:** On Sunday 8 Nov 2026 Huang told Nvidia staff in a memo that he had been "unfaithful" to his wife of 41 years, as the company announced an independent board investigation into concerns that he was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort". Reports in the New York Times and Financial Times said US intelligence agencies believe Alice Huang (no relation), a former managing director at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based buyer of Nvidia hardware, had been passing information to Beijing. The board hired Wachtell, Lipton for the inquiry and said it had "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. On 11 Nov the NYT reported a second alleged affair, with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's VP of healthcare, who reports directly to him (unconfirmed by Nvidia, Huang or Powell); the WSJ then reported the board was preparing to oust him, and the board met late into Wednesday evening.
- **Biggest twist:** Megaspeed was already under fire: in May 2026 short seller Culper Research alleged it was secretly funded by Alibaba through shell companies and helped Nvidia chips reach China. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing; Nvidia says it found no diversion. A trade-war storyline turned into a personal one.
- **Why it could go viral:** The world's most famous CEO, a spy, an affair, a leather jacket and $500bn+ of market value gone in a week.
- **Why our audience cares:** Key-person risk in its purest form: Nvidia has no board chair and no public succession plan, so one man's private life moved the world's biggest stock.
- **Financial results:** Shares **−4.27%** on Mon 9 Nov (close $178.67, ABC News/CNBC), down again ~4% on 10 Nov, then **−8 to −9%** after hours on 11 Nov (Forbes, CNBC). Market value lost during the week: **more than $500bn** (reported). Espionage claims: **alleged** (intelligence officials via press; no charges). Board decision: **unknown at time of writing**.

### A1-2 · Viral 88 · Evidence High · Alleged
**FIRST AN EMAIL FROM THE "CEO", THEN A VIDEO CALL WITH HIS AI-CLONED FACE AND VOICE, THEN A "LAWYER" READING FROM A HANDWRITTEN SCRIPT. PROSECUTORS SAY THREE MEN TOOK $129 MILLION THIS WAY.**
- **Hook:** Hospitals, a bank and a mutual fund wired millions to a boss and a lawyer who never existed.
- **What happened:** Manhattan federal prosecutors unsealed an indictment (reported 5–6 Nov 2026) charging Jacob Soall (41), Sharon Hazan (54) and Guy Sheetrit (42) with wire fraud, aggravated identity theft and money laundering. Prosecutors say that from July 2024 to February 2026 the group emailed accounting staff posing as their CEO about a "confidential deal", then set up calls where AI mimicked the executive's voice and sometimes face, and handed the employee to a fake lawyer at a real major law firm. Victims named in reporting include hospital systems in Missouri and California, a Rhode Island bank, a UK fintech, a Massachusetts mutual-fund company and a Florida pet-food company. Money was routed through accounts in the US, Spain, Portugal, Greece, the UAE, Singapore and elsewhere, then converted to crypto.
- **Biggest twist:** The high-tech part was the face; the closer was low-tech. Prosecutors say Soall played UK lawyers using a handwritten script titled "Lawyer Call".
- **Why it could go viral:** Everyone has a boss and an inbox; "the boss was fake, so was the lawyer, the money was real" lands instantly.
- **Why our audience cares:** Any company with an accounts team is a target. A call-back rule to a known number would have stopped it.
- **Financial results:** Alleged theft **~$129M** (prosecutors). Example losses: a Missouri hospital **~$4.8M** and a California hospital **~$2.97M** (reported; outlets differ). All three presumed innocent; extradition from Spain and the UK pending.

## A3. EXTRAORDINARY BUSINESS MODELS (worldwide)

### A3-1 · Model 86 · Evidence Medium · Partially verified
**TWO-MINUTE SOAP OPERAS SHOT ON A PHONE-SIZED BUDGET, WITH A PAYWALL AT EVERY CLIFFHANGER: THIS APP IS HEADING FOR $1 BILLION IN REVENUE AND ITS FIRST PROFIT**
- **Hook:** Billionaire werewolves and secret-heiress revenge plots, sold one minute at a time.
- **The money machine:** ReelShort (Crazy Maple Studio, majority-owned by China's COL Group) makes vertical "micro-dramas": dozens of 1–2 minute episodes per series. The first episodes are free; the rest unlock with coins or a subscription, so every cliffhanger is a checkout. Growth is bought with heavy user-acquisition ads, and the next lever is distribution through telcos (AIS in Thailand, Globe in the Philippines, Vidio in Indonesia) so that payment bypasses app-store fees.
- **Numbers:** Revenue **$97M (2023) → $785M (2025)**; forecast **~$1.05bn in 2026, +34%** (Media Partners Asia estimate). COL Group filing: **RMB 5.72bn (~$805M) 2025 gross revenue**. **~$12M net loss 2025 → ~$40M net profit 2026** (MPA projection). North America **59%** of revenue; LatAm + APAC ~60% of ~70M monthly users but 19% of revenue (MPA estimate). Q1 2026 in-app revenue **~$140M**, roughly tied with DramaBox (Sensor Tower).
- **Biggest twist:** The money is in America even though most viewers aren't: US users pay, the rest of the world mostly watches.
- **Business lesson:** Sell the next minute, not the whole story: a paywall placed at peak curiosity converts better than a subscription pitch.
- **Monetization opportunity:** Produce micro-drama ads for brands (story-driven vertical series instead of one-off spots); a content series breaking down micro-drama hooks; sell "cliffhanger paywall" structure to course or newsletter clients.
- **Slovenia/EU angle:** none (EU micro-drama production is still small, which is the opening).
- **Dates:** ReelShort launched 2022 · MPA report Aug 2026 · Indonesia (Vidio) deal late Aug 2026.
- **Sources:** [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/08/microdrama-reelshort-revenue-profit-1237027492/) · [Media Partners Asia (report page)](https://www.media-partners-asia.com/reports/reelshort-%2F-crazy-maple-studio%3A-inside-the-us%241b-micro-drama-machine) · [Indian Television](https://indiantelevision.com/iworld/reelshort-set-to-cross-1-bn-as-micro-drama-market-gathers-pace/) · [ContentAsia (Vidio deal)](https://www.contentasia.tv/news/vidio-seals-landmark-short-drama-deal-reelshort-indonesian-streaming-leader-adds-200-titles-co)

### A3-2 · Model 83 · Evidence Medium · Partially verified
**FIVE ENGINEERS QUIT DJI IN THE PANDEMIC. THEIR 3D-PRINTER COMPANY NOW SELLS MORE THAN ANYONE ON EARTH, AND IT PAYS STRANGERS TO GIVE THE DESIGNS AWAY FREE.**
- **Hook:** The printer is the product, but the free model library is the moat.
- **The money machine:** Bambu Lab (Shenzhen) sells easy, fast consumer 3D printers plus filament and accessories. Its MakerWorld site gives away millions of printable designs; designers are paid from a points/cash reward budget (reported in the nine-figure RMB range a year), and can also sell models or crowdfund. Every free download is a reason to buy a Bambu printer and more filament: a razor-and-blades model where the "blades" are designs made by the community.
- **Numbers:** Revenue **> RMB 10bn (~$1.4bn) in 2025** (reported, not audited). **No. 1** in 2025 shipments of sub-$2,500 printers, **37% share** (TechNode, citing industry data). MakerWorld **~10M monthly active users** end-2025 (company). Top designers **> RMB 500,000/yr** (reported). Profit: **unknown**.
- **Biggest twist:** The company spends money paying creators to give away designs, and that is exactly why the hardware sells.
- **Business lesson:** Fund the community that makes your product useful; free content made by others can be the cheapest sales channel.
- **Monetization opportunity:** Content series on "the free library behind the printer"; copy the model for any hardware/consumable client (templates, presets, recipes paid to creators); 3D-print product businesses (see CruiseCup, 2026-11-11-A2-1) run on these machines.
- **Slovenia/EU angle:** none.
- **Dates:** founded Nov 2020 (team left DJI Jul 2020) · 2025 results reported early 2026 · TechNode profile 30 Oct 2026 (R1 laser cutter launched Sep 2026).
- **Sources:** [TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/10/30/how-bambu-lab-turned-3d-printing-into-a-consumer-hit/) · [3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/324181/bambu-lab-says-2025-was-a-breakout-year-10-million-monthly-users-and-real-business-growth/) · [EqualOcean (founding)](https://equalocean.com/news/2024010520422-bambu-lab-consumer-grade-3d-printing-manufacturer-nears-cny-1-5-billion-annual) · [The Bamboo Works](https://thebambooworks.com/printing-the-future-how-chinas-consumer-3d-printing-boom-is-reshaping-global-markets/)
