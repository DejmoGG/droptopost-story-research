### A1-2 · Viral 88 · Evidence High · Verified (jury verdict)
**THEY SOLD INVESTORS 60,000 MORE COWS THAN THEY OWNED. A TEXAS JURY HAS NOW CONVICTED TWO BROTHERS OVER THE $240 MILLION "GHOST HERD".**
- **Hook:** Thousands of people bought cattle on paper; the cattle weren't there.
- **What happened:** Agridime, a Texas company, sold "cattle contracts" promising investors fixed returns when the animals were fattened and sold. Prosecutors say it collected more than **$240M** from 2,200+ victims while selling about **60,000 more head of cattle than it held**, paid old investors with new money, and overstated its herd to get financing. On 10 Nov 2026, after a six-day trial in Fort Worth, a jury convicted co-owner Joshua Link (wire fraud conspiracy, wire fraud, money laundering) and his brother Jason Link (same charges). Jurors also found the brothers billed Agridime for feed and yard services their own Shady Brook Ranch never provided, so the company paid twice (**>$2.7M**).
- **Biggest twist:** Joshua Link was a fugitive when indicted in Feb 2026; the FBI caught him at Los Angeles International Airport.
- **Why it could go viral:** "Ghost herd" is a perfect image, and rural cattle investing sounds like the safest thing in the world.
- **Why our audience cares:** Physical assets feel safe because you can picture them. Nobody went to count the cows.
- **Financial results:** Collected **>$240M** (prosecutors); double-billed feed **>$2.7M** (jury finding); SEC had already called it a Ponzi scheme in **Dec 2023**. Sentencing **4 Mar 2027**. Other defendants: operations director Jed Wood pleaded guilty (Mar 2026); others' outcomes unknown.
- **Audience signals:** not measured (syndicated across all Fox local stations).
- **Original visuals:** none found (DOJ release text; FOX 4 Dallas–Fort Worth coverage).
- **Dates:** scheme to 2023 · indictment Feb 2026 · verdict 10 Nov 2026 (announced 12 Nov).

- **Why it could go viral:** Brothers, MIT, 12 seconds, $25M, and a "not guilty" that sounds like a loophole.
- **Why our audience cares:** Code isn't law, and neither is ethics: what's legal in unregulated markets is still being decided case by case.

## A3. EXTRAORDINARY BUSINESS MODELS (worldwide)

### A3-1 · Model 80 · Evidence Medium · Partially verified
**INDIA'S RIDE-HAILING UPSTART TAKES ZERO COMMISSION FROM DRIVERS. INSTEAD, EACH PAYS ₹5–29 A DAY. IT NOW HAS TWICE UBER'S USERS IN INDIA AND A $3 BILLION VALUATION.**
- **Hook:** It turned the gig-platform model upside down: the driver keeps the fare, the app charges rent.
- **The money machine:** Rapido started with bike taxis (cheap, fast in traffic). For autos (2024) and cabs (2023) it launched with no per-ride commission: drivers buy a daily subscription (₹5–29, reported) and keep the whole fare. Drivers move over because they keep more, riders follow because more drivers mean shorter waits, and Rapido earns predictable fees plus delivery and bike-taxi commissions. Uber (Feb 2025) and Ola copied the subscription model.
- **Numbers:** FY25 operating revenue **₹934 crore (+44%)**, net loss **₹258 crore** (down from ₹371 crore) (Entrackr, from filings). Subscription revenue **₹275 crore**, bike-taxi commissions **₹277 crore**, delivery **₹340 crore** (FY25). Raised **$240M at a $3bn post-money valuation** (May 2026, Prosus-led; company statement via TechCrunch). Monthly users **~2x Uber, ~4x Ola** in India (reported analytics). Market share: company claims, conflicting (bike taxi 56–70%). FY26 results: **unknown**.
- **Biggest twist:** The "no commission" pitch has already become an industry standard, but Rapido's biggest revenue line is still old-fashioned delivery commissions.
- **Business lesson:** If your suppliers hate your take rate, charge them a flat fee instead: it can win the supply side of a marketplace almost overnight.
- **Monetization opportunity:** Content series on "the app that charges drivers rent"; pitch the flat-fee model to marketplace clients (salons, tutors, trades: a monthly seat instead of a cut per booking); a playbook for local delivery or booking platforms in smaller markets.
- **Slovenia/EU angle:** none (EU platform-work rules may make a flat-fee model easier to defend than commissions).
- **Dates:** zero-commission cabs 2023, autos 2024 · FY25 results late 2025 · $3bn round 15 May 2026.
- **Sources:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/indian-uber-rival-rapido-raises-240m-at-3b-valuation/) · [Entrackr (FY25)](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/rapido-joins-rs-1000-cr-income-club-in-fy25-delivery-biz-outpaces-ride-hailing-11014839) · [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/rapido-raises-240-mn-at-3-bn-valuation-to-expand-footprint) · [Open Magazine (Uber squeeze)](https://openthemagazine.com/business/ubers-india-squeeze-rapido-surges-as-the-driver-battle-intensifies) · [OfficeChai (users vs Uber)](https://officechai.com/stories/rapido-now-has-twice-as-many-monthly-users-as-uber-and-4x-as-many-as-ola/)
