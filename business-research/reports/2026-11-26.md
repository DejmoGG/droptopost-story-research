## A1. SCAMS & SCANDALS

### A1-1 · Viral 88 · Evidence High · Alleged (indictment; DOJ release)
**VICTIMS HIRED HIM TO BEAT THE HACKERS. PROSECUTORS SAY HE SECRETLY PAID THE HACKERS, THEN SENT THE VICTIMS A BIGGER BILL.**
- **Hook:** A Kansas business paid about $720,000 for "data recovery". Prosecutors say about $445,000 went to the ransomware gang and the rest was the recovery firm's cut.
- **What happened:** On 25 Nov 2026 the US Attorney's Office in Rhode Island announced the indictment of Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, co-founder of a Florida company that marketed data-recovery services to ransomware victims (TechCrunch describes him as the former CTO of RansomwareHelp). Prosecutors say the company, running since 2020, had no real decryption capability: Palacio negotiated with the gangs himself, bought the keys, and charged clients more than the ransom. The case was built partly through an FBI sting in which an undercover employee posed as the hacker; the company reportedly netted $10,010 from that fake job. He was arrested in Las Vegas on 22 Oct and granted pretrial release on 20 Nov. He faces three counts of wire fraud.
- **Biggest twist:** The "rescue" service and the criminals were, in effect, doing the same deal. The victim just paid twice.
- **Why it could go viral:** A middleman profiting off both sides of a hostage negotiation, caught by an FBI agent playing the hacker.
- **Why our audience cares:** Any business can be hit by ransomware. Ask a recovery vendor exactly how they recover data, and whether they pay ransoms.
- **Financial results:** Kansas case: **~$720,000 charged, ~$445,000 paid to the gang** (prosecutors, via 404 Media). FBI sting profit: **$10,010** (reported). Total take: unknown. Up to **20 years per count** (statutory maximum). All allegations; presumed innocent.
- **Audience signals:** not measured (DOJ, TechCrunch, 404 Media, Social News XYZ on 25 Nov).
- **Original visuals:** none found (DOJ press release; the company's old marketing site, not checked).
- **Dates:** scheme from 2020 · arrest 22 Oct 2026 · indictment announced 25 Nov 2026.
- **Sources:** [US DOJ, District of Rhode Island](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ri/pr/florida-data-recovery-company-co-founder-charged-defrauding-ransomware-victims) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/doj-accuses-former-cyber-executive-of-defrauding-ransomware-victims-by-secretly-paying-hackers) · [404 Media](https://404media.co/ransomware-fraud-attack-fbi-indictment)

### A1-2 · Viral 82 · Evidence High · Verified (jury verdict)
**THE STATE PAID FOR 4,500 STUDENTS WHO NEVER LOGGED IN. THE MAN WHO RAN ENROLLMENT HAS JUST BEEN FOUND GUILTY.**
- **Hook:** An online school was paid per pupil. Prosecutors showed thousands of the pupils were on paper only.
- **What happened:** A federal jury found Phillip Holden, 62, former director of Indiana Virtual School, guilty of fraud on 5 Nov 2026 after a trial that began 27 Oct. Prosecutors said that between 2016 and 2018 Indiana Virtual School and Indiana Virtual Pathways Academy reported more than 4,500 students who weren't attending, and siphoned about $44M in state funding through a web of related companies. Holden was in charge of enrollment, attendance and teacher pay. Former superintendent Percy Clark pleaded guilty in June 2025. A 2019 state audit had put the overpayment at more than $68M, and the state is suing for more than $154M.
- **Biggest twist:** The schools kept "students" on the rolls who hadn't logged in for months, because each name was worth money.
- **Why it could go viral:** Ghost students is an instantly understandable con, with a huge round number of empty seats.
- **Why our audience cares:** Any per-head payment system (per user, per seat, per patient) invites this. Auditors check activity, not sign-ups.
- **Financial results:** **~$44M** alleged loss to the state (indictment, Chalkbeat). State audit: **$68M+** overpaid (2019). State civil claim: **$154M+**. Sentencing date: unknown.
- **Audience signals:** not measured (Chalkbeat, IBJ, WFYI).
- **Original visuals:** none found (2019 state audit report; local TV coverage).
- **Dates:** scheme 2016–2018 · indictment Jan 2024 · verdict 5 Nov 2026 (reported 6 Nov).
- **Sources:** [Chalkbeat Indiana](https://www.chalkbeat.org/indiana/2026/11/06/former-virtual-school-director-guilty-of-fraud-enrollment-scheme/) · [Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/former-indiana-virtual-school-director-found-guilty-of-fraud-in-enrollment-inflation-scheme) · [Chalkbeat (trial opens)](https://www.chalkbeat.org/indiana/2026/10/27/indiana-virtual-school-fraud-trial-begins/)

## A3. EXTRAORDINARY BUSINESS MODELS (worldwide)

### A3-1 · Model 82 · Evidence Medium · Partially verified (FY2025 figures differ slightly by source; HKEX filing not opened)
**30,000 SNACK SHOPS, 11% GROSS MARGIN, AND A STOCK THAT JUMPED 70% ON DAY ONE.**
- **Hook:** China's biggest snack retailer sells sweets cheaper than supermarkets and still makes billions of yuan.
- **The money machine:** Busy Ming (formed in 2023 by merging Busy Snacks and Zhao Yiming Snacks) runs small franchised discount snack shops in towns and neighbourhoods. It buys in bulk straight from factories, skips the supermarket's slotting fees and middlemen, and prices well below hypermarkets. The margin per bag is thin, so the model only works at enormous volume and store count, with franchisees carrying the store costs.
- **Numbers:** FY2025 **revenue RMB 45.0bn (+60%)**; **net profit RMB 2.2–2.3bn (+155–181%, sources differ)**; **gross margin 9.3% → 11.5%** (reported via Quartr, Tiger, Dolphin Research). **30,000+ stores** (China Daily via The Star, Aug 2026). IPO Jan 2026: **raised HK$3.5bn, +69–75% first day** (Caixin, The Standard).
- **Biggest twist:** A grocery format most investors would call low-margin produced one of Hong Kong's hottest IPOs of the year.
- **Business lesson:** If you can't beat incumbents on margin, beat them on purchasing power and store density.
- **Monetization opportunity:** A short video series on "how a 1-yuan snack funds a 30,000-store empire"; advise EU food brands on selling into Chinese discount channels; a franchise-economics explainer for clients.
- **Slovenia/EU angle:** none.
- **Dates:** merger 2023 · IPO late Jan 2026 · FY2025 results 31 Mar 2026 · 30,000 stores Aug 2026.
- **Sources:** [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-01-28/chinas-largest-discount-snack-chain-skyrockets-in-hong-kong-trading-debut-102409007.html) · [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/322875/Chinese-snack-retailer-Busy-Ming-surges-75pc-in-Hong-Kong-debut) · [The Star / China Daily](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/08/13/chinas-snack-retailers-maximise-growth-potential) · [KrASIA (founder)](https://kr-asia.com/how-busy-ming-rose-behind-a-low-profile-but-resolute-founder)
