## A1. SCAMS & SCANDALS

### A1-1 · Viral 88 · Evidence High · Verified (NBA findings and penalties; criminal case closed by guilty plea)
**A GREEN-BANKING STARTUP PAID AN NBA SUPERSTAR $28 MILLION FOR A SPONSORSHIP. THE STARTUP'S CO-FOUNDER IS NOW SERVING 14 YEARS FOR FRAUD, AND THE LEAGUE HAS SUSPENDED THE TEAM'S BILLIONAIRE OWNER FOR A YEAR.**
- **Hook:** A climate-friendly fintech, a $28M endorsement deal, a $50M investment from the team owner and the biggest penalty in NBA history.
- **What happened:** In 2021 Kawhi Leonard signed a four-year, $28M endorsement deal with Aspiration, a "sustainable" finance startup in which Clippers owner Steve Ballmer had invested $50M. Journalist Pablo Torre reported in Sept 2025 that the payments looked like a way around the salary cap; the NBA opened an investigation, run by law firm Wachtell Lipton. Meanwhile Aspiration went bankrupt and its co-founder Joe Sanberg pleaded guilty (Oct 2025) to defrauding lenders and investors of at least $248M; on 1 Jun 2026 he got 14 years. On 2 Sep 2026 the NBA fined the Clippers $30M, took five first-round picks, suspended Ballmer for one year from all league and team activity, suspended business president Gillian Zucker for a year and basketball chief Lawrence Frank for six months, and fined Leonard $700,000.
- **Biggest twist:** The NBA found the Clippers set up deals for Leonard with four companies (Aspiration, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, Lockton), and that Ballmer approved the Aspiration deal knowing it was a precondition for Aspiration's team sponsorship. The Clippers call the investigation "heavily biased" and reject the findings.
- **Why it could go viral:** One of the richest men in the world, an NBA star, a fake-looking "green" job and a convicted founder, all in one story.
- **Why our audience cares:** A sponsorship can be a disguised payment, and a "purpose-driven" startup can be cooking its books. The investor (Ballmer, $50M) and the fraud (Sanberg, $248M) sat in the same deal.
- **Financial results:** Endorsement **$28M** over four years (reported). Ballmer investment in Aspiration **$50M** (reported). Fraud **≥$248M** (prosecutors; guilty plea). Penalties: Clippers fine **$30M**, five first-round picks forfeited, Leonard fine **$700,000** (NBA). Ballmer and Leonard deny wrongdoing; the NBA's findings are a league ruling, not a court finding against them.
- **Audience signals:** not measured (covered by ESPN, NBA.com, Al Jazeera, LAist, Salt Lake Tribune).
- **Original visuals:** NBA's published findings (nba.com); Pablo Torre's original podcast episode (Sept 2025); Ballmer/Leonard denials on video from 2025.
- **Dates:** deal 2021 · report Sept 2025 · Sanberg sentenced 1 Jun 2026 · NBA penalties 2 Sep 2026.

### A1-2 · Viral 82 · Evidence High · Alleged (charges; collapse facts verified)
**11,200 SAVERS WERE SOLD "8% FIXED-RATE ISAs" THAT WERE NEVER ISAs. SEVEN YEARS AFTER £251 MILLION VANISHED, EIGHT PEOPLE HAVE BEEN CHARGED, INCLUDING THREE PENSIONERS.**
- **Hook:** A firm regulated by the UK's financial watchdog sold unregulated bonds as safe savings accounts, and the administrators called it a Ponzi scheme.
- **What happened:** London Capital & Finance (LCF) sold mini-bonds to ordinary savers, advertised with returns of up to 8% and wrongly described as fixed-rate ISAs. It collapsed into administration in January 2019; administrators (Smith & Williamson) said in March 2019 that it was a Ponzi scheme and found misrepresentation about the bonds' security and ISA status. An official inquiry led by Dame Elizabeth Gloster found the FCA's approach to its perimeter was "unduly limited". On 16 Nov 2026 the Serious Fraud Office charged eight people: six, including Simon Hume-Kendall (73) and former CEO Michael Andrew Thomson (53), with conspiracy to defraud; Helen Hume-Kendall (64) with concealing criminal property; and an eighth, unnamed, with conspiracy to defraud and breaching a director disqualification undertaking. All are due at Westminster Magistrates' Court on 7 Dec 2026.
- **Biggest twist:** The company itself was FCA-authorised, which is what made savers trust it, but the bonds it sold were not regulated products.
- **Why it could go viral:** "ISA" is the safest word in British savings, and it was used to sell something that wasn't one.
- **Why our audience cares:** A regulated company can still sell you an unregulated product. Check the product, not the logo.
- **Financial results:** Investor losses **~£251M** (SFO estimate; earlier administrators' count ~£237M from 11,625 bondholders). Investors **>11,200** (SFO). Recovery: administrators once warned some victims might get back **as little as 10%** (reported). All defendants are presumed innocent.
- **Audience signals:** not measured (City AM, Money Marketing, Professional Adviser, GB News).
- **Original visuals:** none found (SFO statement on gov.uk; archive LCF ads and website captures from 2018).
- **Dates:** collapse Jan 2019 · Ponzi finding Mar 2019 · charges announced 16 Nov 2026 · first hearing 7 Dec 2026.
- **Sources:** [SFO / gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sfo-charges-eight-over-london-capital-finance-collapse) · [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/sfo-charges-eight-over-251m-london-capital-finance-collapse/) · [Money Marketing](https://www.moneymarketing.co.uk/news/eight-charged-over-251m-london-capital-finance-collapse/) · [GB News](https://www.gbnews.com/money/fraud-lcf-investment-ponzi-scheme) · [Bond Review (2019 Ponzi finding)](https://bondreview.co.uk/2019/03/27/london-capital-finance-was-a-ponzi-scheme-administrators-confirm/?amp=1) · [City AM (Gloster inquiry)](https://www.cityam.com/treasury-fca-failed-to-properly-regulate-london-capital-and-finance/)
